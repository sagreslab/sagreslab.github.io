Yulong Guo developed a miniaturized microfluidic flow sensor in standard CMOS technology, integrating a metal-resistive heater, two bipolar junction transistor (BJT) temperature sensors, and associated readout and signal-processing circuits. By measuring flow-induced temperature differences between upstream and downstream sensors, the system achieves a flow-rate precision of 0.11 µL/min at low flow rates, within a full-scale range of 9 µL/min. Occupying less than 0.2 mm² and consuming approximately 65 mW, the sensor demonstrates the potential for compact, low-cost flow measurement in microfluidic applications. The work also identifies heat conduction through the silicon substrate as a key performance limitation and proposes thermal isolation and further circuit integration to improve efficiency and sensitivity.

Eduardo Pinto investigated the design and optimization of an electromagnetic vibration energy harvester integrated into a military boot, aiming to convert energy from heel strikes into electrical power for wearable devices. His work explored the influence of resonance frequency, magnet positioning, coil design, and load resistance on energy harvesting performance. Through laboratory experiments, he identified an optimal resonance frequency of 13.96 Hz and achieved a peak output power of 83.06 μW under controlled conditions. The results demonstrate the feasibility of harvesting energy from human motion for low-power applications and contribute to the development of autonomous wearable electronics for military and remote-field use.
